Why fractional

Risk needs an owner at the leadership table.

Cybersecurity, technology and artificial intelligence now carry consequences that reach the board. Each requires a senior owner with the authority to set direction and the experience to defend it.

For many institutions, a full-time executive is difficult to justify, slow to recruit and hard to retain. In the absence of one, responsibility is divided among providers and no one owns the outcome.

A fractional leader closes that gap: an experienced executive who knows your institution, serves on your committees and answers to your board and your regulators.

The roles

Two roles. One standard of accountability.

A

Fractional Chief Information Security Officer

Owns the security program

Sets security strategy and is accountable for it to the executive team, the board and regulators.

  • Security strategy, roadmap and budget
  • Oversight of testing, vulnerability management and incident response
  • Regulatory and examination response
  • Board and committee reporting
B

Fractional Chief Technology & AI Risk Officer

Owns the technology and AI risk program

Governs how the institution adopts technology and artificial intelligence, and the authority it extends to vendors, models and agents.

  • Technology and AI risk framework
  • Artificial intelligence governance, inventory and risk tiering
  • Third-party, cloud and platform risk
  • Board and committee reporting

The two roles may be combined in a single mandate.
